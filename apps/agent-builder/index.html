How it works

Build a voice role-play, test it, publish it, invite people, and read the reports.

1. Create a role-play

  • Describe the conversation in plain words on the first screen (who the persona is, the situation, what to assess, roughly how long), or start from a template.
  • Choose Role-play or Assessment, then Create with Tara. A draft takes about 20 seconds.

2. Review the setup

  • Scenario details: the type, the role or situation, and what to assess.
  • Description: what participants read before they start. Keep it short and encouraging.
  • Instructions: how the persona runs the conversation. Participants never see this.
  • Persona: name, title, voice and tone. Changing the voice changes the persona's name everywhere it's used. Follow-up depth also sets the length (Light ≈ 10, Probing ≈ 20, Deep dive ≈ 30 minutes).
  • Skills: what the conversation is scored on. Add skills from the Skill Master, set what a 5 looks like for each, and make the weights total 100%. Then select Mark as reviewed.

3. Questions and settings

  • Potential AI Questions are a plan, not a script: the persona picks and adapts them. Use Generate more for ideas.
  • Conversation settings: how it ends and how many attempts one person gets.
  • Advanced: language, proctoring defaults and recording consent.

4. Test it

  • Test agent starts a real voice call with the persona (up to 5 minutes). Allow the microphone, and use Chrome for the best result.
  • The transcript appears as you talk. Afterwards, Score this test shows how an attempt like yours would be evaluated.

5. Publish

  • Publish freezes this version for participants. A published role-play is locked so results always match what people took.
  • To change it later, use Duplicate to edit, then publish the copy.

6. Invite participants

  • Email Invitation: enter up to 10 addresses. Each person gets their own link and access code. You can edit the invitation text and use placeholders such as {PARTICIPANT_NAME}.
  • Open Link Invitation: one link anyone can use. It's off until you switch it on, and switching it off stops it working at once.
  • Participants choose a time, check their microphone and start. Calls are limited to the times they booked, so the voice lines are never overloaded.

7. Read the reports

  • Reports lists every attempt with its status, score and recommendation. Use search and filters, or Download Excel.
  • Open a report for the score on each skill, quotes from what the participant actually said, and skills marked Not assessed when the conversation didn't reach them.
  • Add your own recommendation and evaluation notes. The AI's recommendation stays alongside yours. Participants never see results.

Good to know

  • The persona never says it is an AI and never mentions scores or skills to the participant.
  • Scores are only given where there is evidence. A skill with no evidence is Not assessed, not 0.
  • Everything you type is saved automatically while the role-play is a draft.
